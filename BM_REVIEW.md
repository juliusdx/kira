## How to use this

Work top to bottom — the sections are ordered by how much a correction buys you.

1. **Inconsistencies** are already bugs: the same English term is rendered two ways in BM, or one BM string covers two different English terms. Pick the right one and the other is a fix, whichever way it goes.
2. **Terminology** is the highest-leverage read. A term used 14 times is checked once here and fixed in 14 places.
3. **Full text** is the careful pass over prompts and explanations.

Write corrections on the `→` line under anything wrong. Leave the rest untouched. Hand the file back and the edits get applied to `seed_content.json`, which is the only real source — this document is generated and is never itself the content.

#### Balancing Off

`l30-balance-off` · BM title: `Mengimbangkan Akaun`

> **Worked example (EN)** Closing an account has two halves that sit on OPPOSITE sides. The balance c/d is written on the smaller side, because its job is to make the two totals equal. The balance b/d then comes down on the larger side — and that is the side the account really has its balance on. Trade Receivables normally ends with a debit balance and Trade Payables with a credit one, but the entry that closes each of them is written on the other side.
> **(BM)** Menutup akaun ada dua bahagian yang berada di sebelah BERTENTANGAN. Baki c/d dicatat di sebelah yang lebih kecil, kerana tugasnya menyamakan kedua-dua jumlah. Baki b/d kemudian dibawa turun di sebelah yang lebih besar — dan itulah sebelah baki sebenar akaun tersebut. Penghutang Perdagangan biasanya berakhir dengan baki debit dan Pemiutang Perdagangan dengan baki kredit, tetapi catatan yang menutup setiap satunya ditulis di sebelah yang satu lagi.

#### Reducing Balance

`l44-reducing-balance` · BM title: `Baki Berkurangan`

> **Worked example (EN)** Reducing balance charges the rate on what the asset is still worth in the books — the carrying amount — not on its cost. So the first year's charge is the largest and every year after it is smaller. Year 1: rate × cost (there is no accumulated depreciation yet). Year 2 onwards: rate × (cost − accumulated depreciation to date). The trap is applying the rate to cost every year: that is the straight-line answer wearing a reducing-balance question.
> **(BM)** Baki berkurangan mengenakan kadar ke atas nilai aset yang masih tinggal dalam buku — amaun bawaan — bukan ke atas kosnya. Jadi caj tahun pertama adalah yang terbesar dan setiap tahun selepasnya lebih kecil. Tahun 1: kadar × kos (belum ada susut nilai terkumpul). Tahun 2 dan seterusnya: kadar × (kos − susut nilai terkumpul setakat ini). Perangkapnya ialah mengenakan kadar ke atas kos setiap tahun: itu jawapan garis lurus dalam soalan baki berkurangan.

#### Selling the Asset

`l45-disposal` · BM title: `Menjual Aset`

> **Worked example (EN)** When an asset is sold, ask one question: did it fetch more or less than it was still worth in the books? Carrying amount = cost − accumulated depreciation. Proceeds above that is a profit on disposal; below it is a loss. Both go to the Income Statement. In the ledger a Disposal account collects three things — the cost (debit), the accumulated depreciation (credit) and the sale proceeds (credit) — and whatever is left over is the profit or the loss.
> **(BM)** Apabila sesuatu aset dijual, tanya satu soalan: adakah ia laku lebih atau kurang daripada nilai yang masih tinggal dalam buku? Amaun bawaan = kos − susut nilai terkumpul. Hasil jualan melebihi itu ialah untung atas pelupusan; kurang daripadanya ialah rugi. Kedua-duanya masuk ke Penyata Pendapatan. Dalam lejar, Akaun Pelupusan mengumpul tiga perkara — kos (debit), susut nilai terkumpul (kredit) dan hasil jualan (kredit) — dan bakinya ialah untung atau rugi itu.

#### Cash Book vs Bank Statement

`l17-cash-book-vs-bank` · BM title: `Buku Tunai lwn Penyata Bank`

> **Worked example (EN)** The cash book and the bank statement disagree for two different reasons, and each is handled differently. #### Which Side?

`l19-control-sides` · BM title: `Sebelah Mana?`

> **Worked example (EN)** A control account squeezes a whole ledger into one T-account, so its total can be checked against the individual accounts. The Sales Ledger Control Account behaves like one giant debtor: it is DEBITED with opening balance, credit sales and dishonoured cheques, and CREDITED with anything that reduces what customers owe — money received, discount allowed, returns inwards, bad debts, and contra set-offs. The Purchases Ledger Control Account is the mirror image: credited with credit purchases, debited with payments, discount received and returns outwards.
> **(BM)** Akaun kawalan memampatkan keseluruhan lejar ke dalam satu akaun T, supaya jumlahnya boleh disemak dengan akaun individu. Akaun Kawalan Lejar Jualan berkelakuan seperti satu penghutang gergasi: ia DIDEBIT dengan baki awal, jualan kredit dan cek tak layan, dan DIKREDIT dengan apa sahaja yang mengurangkan hutang pelanggan — wang diterima, diskaun diberi, pulangan masuk, hutang lapuk, dan kontra. Akaun Kawalan Lejar Belian ialah cerminannya: dikredit dengan belian kredit, didebit dengan bayaran, diskaun diterima dan pulangan keluar.

### Correction of Errors & Suspense

`t14-suspense` · BM title: `Pembetulan Kesilapan & Akaun Penyelesaian`

#### Six Errors the Trial Balance Cannot See

`l21-error-types` · BM title: `Enam Kesilapan Yang Imbangan Duga Tidak Nampak`

> **Worked example (EN)** A trial balance that agrees is not proof the books are right. Six errors keep both sides equal and slip straight through: OMISSION (left out entirely), COMMISSION (right kind of account, wrong person), PRINCIPLE (wrong class of account — an asset treated as an expense), ORIGINAL ENTRY (the same wrong figure used on both sides), COMPLETE REVERSAL (debit and credit swapped), and COMPENSATING (two errors of the same size cancelling out). Only a one-sided error — a missing posting, or a column added up wrongly — makes the trial balance disagree, and that is what a suspense account is for.
> **(BM)** Imbangan duga yang seimbang bukan bukti buku itu betul. Enam kesilapan mengekalkan kedua-dua belah sama dan lolos terus: PENINGGALAN (tertinggal sepenuhnya), KOMISEN (jenis akaun betul, orang salah), PRINSIP (kelas akaun salah — aset dianggap belanja), CATATAN ASAL (angka salah yang sama digunakan di kedua-dua belah), TERBALIK SEPENUHNYA (debit dan kredit bertukar), dan SALING MENGIMBANGI (dua kesilapan sama besar yang membatalkan). Hanya kesilapan sebelah — catatan yang tertinggal, atau lajur yang salah campur — membuat imbangan duga tidak seimbang, dan itulah gunanya akaun penyelesaian.

#### Profit Without a Ledger

`l24-capital-comparison` · BM title: `Untung Tanpa Lejar`

> **Worked example (EN)** Many small traders keep no double entry at all, so there is no income statement to read the profit from. It has to be reasoned out instead: capital is whatever the business owns minus what it owes (a Statement of Affairs), and any GROWTH in capital over the year must have come from profit. Two adjustments finish it — drawings are ADDED back, because money the owner took out was still profit earned; capital introduced is DEDUCTED, because that growth came from the owner's pocket, not from trading.
> **(BM)** Ramai peniaga kecil langsung tidak menyimpan catatan bergu, jadi tiada penyata pendapatan untuk membaca untung. Ia perlu ditaakul: modal ialah apa yang perniagaan miliki tolak apa yang dihutang (Penyata Hal Ehwal), dan sebarang PERTAMBAHAN modal sepanjang tahun mesti datang daripada untung. Dua pelarasan melengkapkannya — ambilan DITAMBAH semula, kerana wang yang diambil pemilik tetap untung yang diperoleh; modal dimasukkan DITOLAK, kerana pertambahan itu datang daripada poket pemilik, bukan daripada perniagaan.

#### Capital or Current?

`l28-capital-vs-current` · BM title: `Modal atau Semasa?`

> **Worked example (EN)** With two or more owners, one capital account is no longer enough — you must be able to see what each partner is entitled to. So each partner gets TWO accounts. The CAPITAL account holds their permanent investment and barely moves. The CURRENT account holds everything that changes year to year: credited with their share of profit, interest on capital and any salary; debited with drawings and interest on drawings. Anything a partner earns from the business is a credit to their current account; anything they take out is a debit.
> **(BM)** Dengan dua atau lebih pemilik, satu akaun modal tidak lagi mencukupi — anda mesti dapat melihat hak setiap pekongsi. Jadi setiap pekongsi mendapat DUA akaun. Akaun MODAL memegang pelaburan tetap mereka dan jarang berubah. Akaun SEMASA memegang segala yang berubah dari tahun ke tahun: dikredit dengan bahagian untung, faedah atas modal dan sebarang gaji; didebit dengan ambilan dan faedah atas ambilan. Apa sahaja yang pekongsi peroleh daripada perniagaan ialah kredit ke akaun semasa; apa sahaja yang mereka ambil ialah debit.

#### Direct, Indirect or Neither

`l35-manufacturing-costs` · BM title: `Langsung, Tak Langsung atau Bukan Kilang`

> **Worked example (EN)** A cost is DIRECT if you can trace it to the product itself — the material in it, the wages of the person who made it, a royalty paid on each unit. A cost is a factory OVERHEAD if it keeps the factory running but cannot be traced to one unit. A cost that has nothing to do with the factory never enters the manufacturing account at all.
> **(BM)** Sesuatu kos adalah LANGSUNG jika ia boleh dikesan terus kepada produk itu sendiri — bahan di dalamnya, gaji orang yang membuatnya, royalti yang dibayar bagi setiap unit. Sesuatu kos ialah OVERHED kilang jika ia mengekalkan operasi kilang tetapi tidak boleh dikesan kepada satu unit. Kos yang tiada kaitan dengan kilang langsung tidak masuk ke dalam akaun pengilangan.
